# Academic context profiles

Read only the profile that matches the requested passage. Apply the shared evidence
rules first; these profiles change emphasis, not factual standards.

## Abstract and introduction

- Establish the problem, gap, approach, and strongest supported result quickly.
- Keep only limitations needed to define the paper's actual scope.
- Do not preload hypothetical reviewer objections or methods not used in the work.
- Prefer one concrete contribution over a list of inflated contribution labels.

## Methods

- Name the operation, input, output, and evaluation procedure concretely.
- Keep legitimate terms such as margin, probe, calibration, and confidence interval
  when the method defines and uses them.
- Do not add a control, probe, statistical test, or robustness procedure merely
  because a conventional paper might contain one.

## Results

- State observed and comparative results directly under the evaluated conditions.
- Preserve reported uncertainty, sample size, units, and comparison direction.
- Keep interpretation separate from measurement. Do not turn association into
  causation or evaluated performance into universal generalization.
- Do not append a generic caution after every result.

## Tables and captions

- Preserve every supplied value and its relationship to row and column labels.
- Include uncertainty or significance only when supplied, computed, or required.
- Make the caption identify the measure and setting; do not use it to advertise the
  method or invent a second interpretation of the table.
- Apply the table and formatting rules linked directly from the Skill.

## Discussion and conclusion

- Interpret the evidence at the scope the study supports.
- State a material limitation once, where it changes interpretation.
- Remove catalogs of speculative weaknesses, generic future work, and final-paragraph
  self-negation.
- End with the established result and its significance, not a new disclaimer.
