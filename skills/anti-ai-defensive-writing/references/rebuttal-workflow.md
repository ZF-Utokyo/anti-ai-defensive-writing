# Rebuttal and reviewer response

Use this workflow for rebuttals, author responses, responses to meta-reviews, and
revision letters. Keep the reviewer comment, response text, and manuscript changes
as separate evidence surfaces. Do not treat a request to answer reviewers as
permission to rewrite the paper or promise new work.

## Build a comment-to-evidence ledger

For each comment, record internally:

- **Comment ID:** reviewer and comment identifier.
- **Actual concern:** the narrow question or objection that needs an answer.
- **Affected claim:** the manuscript claim whose support or scope is challenged.
- **Existing evidence:** the result, method, citation, figure, table, or explanation
  that bears on the concern.
- **Evidence location:** the exact section, page, line, figure, table, or appendix.
- **Response path:** already addressed, misunderstanding, valid gap, or optional
  suggestion.
- **Authorized action:** a clarification or change the author has actually approved.
- **Status:** unresolved, drafted, verified, or completed.

Keep the ledger internal unless the user asks to see it. Never fill a missing
location, experiment, result, or commitment by inference.

## Route each comment

1. Restate the concern narrowly without intensifying it.
2. Determine whether it affects the validity or scope of a claim the paper actually
   makes.
3. Locate the supplied evidence before drafting a response.
4. Choose one response path:
   - **Already addressed:** answer directly and give the exact location.
   - **Misunderstanding:** clarify the paper's actual claim and evidence without
     attacking the reviewer.
   - **Valid gap:** acknowledge the precise gap and state only an approved repair.
   - **Optional suggestion:** explain the current scope and avoid presenting an
     unneeded experiment as required for validity.

Treat a requested experiment, baseline, dataset, or analysis as necessary only when
the current evidence cannot support a claim the paper makes. Do not assume that a
newer model, more complex method, SOTA result, positive result, or reviewer-preferred
method is required without that connection.

## Draft an evidence-grounded response

Use this order for each response:

1. **Direct answer:** answer the concern in the first sentence.
2. **Evidence and location:** identify the exact existing support.
3. **Authorized action:** state a verified clarification or approved change, if any.

Remain respectful without praise inflation, automatic agreement, or score-oriented
language. Do not use "we completely agree" as a substitute for analysis. Do not
promise a new experiment, dataset, analysis, citation, or paper change unless the
author has approved it and the work or revision supports the statement. Mark an
unapproved action as a decision needed from the author, not as future work.

Preserve the reviewer's meaning when quoting or paraphrasing. Do not manufacture a
weaker version of the comment merely to make it easier to answer.

## Handle revision letters

Use the same ledger, but require every claimed manuscript change to map to the
revised paper:

- connect each reviewer comment to the response;
- show the exact changed text or describe the concrete change;
- give the verified location in the revised manuscript;
- distinguish completed changes from proposed or declined actions;
- never write "we added," "we clarified," or "we evaluated" unless the supplied
  revision confirms it.

A rebuttal primarily maps comments to existing evidence and permitted commitments.
A revision letter maps comments to completed manuscript changes. Do not collapse
those two evidence standards.

## Run a response integrity pass

Confirm that:

1. every consequential comment has a response or an explicit unresolved status;
2. every factual statement and location maps to supplied material;
3. every commitment is authorized and every claimed completed change exists;
4. no unsupported experiment, number, citation, or interpretation was introduced;
5. the response is direct and professional without capitulation or hostility;
6. reviewer language and promises did not leak into the manuscript as new facts.

For Draft or Clean requests, return the response text first and mention only
material unresolved decisions. For Audit requests, report the comment ID, evidence
gap or unsupported commitment, consequence, and smallest repair. Do not predict score
changes or optimize the response around reviewer manipulation.
